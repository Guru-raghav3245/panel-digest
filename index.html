Why We're Making This

Here's the problem: newspapers are built for adults who already know how to read them. Dense columns, no pictures, a wall of text per story. No wonder most students never get into the habit.

So we draw the story instead. One news event, six panels, done in whatever art style fits that story best. We're not trying to make current affairs “easier.” We're trying to make something a student actually wants to open.

And we don't reuse the same look for every issue. Each one gets its own art style, so flipping through the catalog feels like picking up a different comic each time, not filling in the same template with new headlines. Every issue also comes with a quiz, so reading the story is only half of it — you have to actually get it to move on.

Every issue does two things: a six-panel explainer, and a quiz that checks if it stuck. Same story, told twice, in two different ways.

Analytics

Why We're Betting On This Format

We looked for a study on comics and Olympiad-style GK exams specifically. Couldn't find one — that's actually part of why we're building this catalog. So here's the closest thing we could find instead: three studies on vocabulary, motivation, and test scores from comic-based learning. Different subjects, but the same mechanics we're using — an explainer plus a quiz.

Three studies, three subjects, one pattern: comics move the number that matters.

Vocabulary

Comics Use More Rare Words Than Children's Books

Here's something that surprises people: comics sit closer to adult books than to kids' books when you count rare words per 1,000. That's the raw material a real vocabulary-building quiz needs. It's also why a comic can stand in for a newspaper instead of a picture book — the words in it are already adult-level.

Hayes, D. P. & Ahrens, M. (1988). Vocabulary simplification for children: a special case of “motherese.” Journal of Child Language, 15(2). · Cambridge Core
Attention & motivation

Kids Got More Motivated When The Lesson Was A Comic

A classroom trial swapped a text handout for a digital comic on social studies. Motivation went up, and the number of students who actually hit the lesson's targets jumped from a small handful to almost everyone.

Before comic After comic
Effect of a social-values-based digital comic on motivation and learning outcomes, Class X, MA Al Islam Telaga Biru. World Journal of Advanced Research and Reviews. · WJARR
Knowledge-check scores

Test Scores Nearly Doubled After A Comic-Based Module

A 50-question multiple-choice test, before and after a four-week comic-based module. This one's physics, not current affairs, but the test format is the closest match to a quiz like ours.

Badeo, J. M. O. & Ong Kian Koc, B. C. U. (2021). Use of comic-based learning module in physics in enhancing students' achievement and motivation. Science Education International, 32(2), 131–136. · ERIC
